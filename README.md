# Retail Growth & Customer Retention Analytics Platform

## Project Overview

This project analyzes 500,000+ e-commerce transactions from the UCI Online Retail dataset to identify customer value, retention patterns, churn risk, product revenue drivers, and cross-sell opportunities.

The goal of the project is to turn raw transaction-level data into business recommendations that could support growth, retention, merchandising, and customer lifecycle strategy.

The final deliverables include:

- Python analytics pipeline
- Cleaned and processed datasets
- SQLite database generation
- SQL example queries
- RFM customer segmentation
- Cohort retention analysis
- Churn-risk scoring model
- Market basket analysis
- Power BI dashboard
- Consulting-style case study

---

## Business Questions

This project answers five core business questions:

1. Which customers generate the most revenue?
2. Which customer segments are at risk of churn?
3. How well do customer cohorts retain after their first purchase?
4. Which products drive the most revenue and order volume?
5. Which product combinations create cross-sell or bundling opportunities?

## Methodology

### 1. Data Cleaning

The raw transaction data was cleaned by:

- Removing cancelled transactions from sales analysis
- Removing rows with invalid quantity or unit price
- Standardizing column names
- Creating revenue fields
- Separating customer-level transactions from full transaction records
- Preserving cancelled transactions separately for cancellation analysis

Two cleaned transaction datasets were created:

- `transactions_clean_full.csv`: used for revenue, product, and country analysis
- `transactions_clean_customer.csv`: used for customer-level segmentation, retention, and churn modeling

The full cleaned transaction file is generated by the pipeline but excluded from GitHub due to file size limits.

---

### 2. Revenue and Product Analysis

Monthly revenue, product-level revenue, product quantity, average order value, and country-level revenue were calculated.

Product analysis filtered out non-product charges such as postage, manual adjustments, and fees. Products with very low order counts were also excluded from the primary product opportunity chart to avoid overemphasizing one-off outliers.

---

### 3. RFM Customer Segmentation

Customers were segmented using RFM analysis:

- **Recency:** how recently the customer purchased
- **Frequency:** how often the customer purchased
- **Monetary value:** how much revenue the customer generated

Customers were classified into business-friendly segments, including:

- Champions
- Loyal Customers
- High-Value At Risk
- New or Recent Customers
- Low-Value Inactive
- Lost or Dormant
- Needs Attention
- At Risk

---

### 4. Cohort Retention Analysis

Customers were grouped by their first purchase month. Monthly cohort retention was calculated to show how many customers returned in later months.

This analysis helps identify where customers drop off and where lifecycle marketing efforts may be most valuable.

---

### 5. Churn-Risk Scoring

A churn-risk model was built using customer-level behavioral features, including:

- Recency
- Frequency
- Monetary value
- Total items purchased
- Unique products purchased
- Average line revenue
- Active months
- Revenue per order
- Items per order
- Orders per active month

A random forest model was trained to classify customers as likely to churn based on whether they purchased again in a future observation window.

Model performance:

```text
Accuracy: 0.662
AUC: 0.741
Precision: 0.580
Recall: 0.768
```

The model prioritized recall, which is appropriate for retention campaigns where missing an at-risk customer can be more costly than contacting some customers unnecessarily.

---

### 6. Market Basket Analysis

Market basket analysis was performed using association rules to identify products frequently purchased together.

The analysis used:

- Support
- Confidence
- Lift

These metrics were used to identify potential cross-sell, bundling, and product placement opportunities.

## Key Findings

### 1. Revenue increased strongly into Q4

Revenue generally increased in the later months of the dataset, with November 2011 representing the strongest revenue month. December 2011 was treated as a partial month and was not interpreted as a full month-over-month decline.

### 2. Champion customers drive the majority of revenue

Champion customers generated nearly 65% of total revenue. This suggests that customer retention efforts should prioritize protecting and expanding the highest-value customer relationships.

### 3. Retention drops quickly after the first purchase

The cohort retention analysis showed a sharp decline after the first purchase month. This indicates that improving second-purchase behavior may be one of the highest-impact growth opportunities.

### 4. High-risk customers create a targeted reactivation opportunity

The churn-risk scoring framework identified customers with high historical value but elevated risk of inactivity. These customers are strong candidates for targeted reactivation campaigns.

### 5. Repeatable product revenue is concentrated in a small group of products

After removing non-product charges and one-off outliers, product revenue was concentrated among a small set of repeatable products, especially decorative and home goods.

### 6. Product bundles can support cross-sell strategy

Market basket rules identified product combinations with high lift and confidence. These combinations can support bundling, recommendation systems, and merchandising strategy.

---

## Business Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Prioritize high-value at-risk customers

Customers with strong historical value but elevated churn risk should receive priority reactivation campaigns, such as personalized offers, targeted emails, or loyalty incentives.

### Recommendation 2: Improve second-purchase conversion

Because retention drops sharply after the first purchase month, the business should create a post-purchase lifecycle campaign designed to encourage a second order within 30 to 60 days.

### Recommendation 3: Protect Champion customers

Champion customers generate the largest share of revenue. The business should maintain loyalty programs, early product access, and personalized engagement for this segment.

### Recommendation 4: Use product bundles to increase average order value

Products with strong association-rule lift and confidence should be tested as bundles, cross-sell recommendations, or co-promoted items.

### Recommendation 5: Improve inventory planning around seasonal demand

Revenue increased substantially into Q4, suggesting the need for stronger inventory planning, staffing, and promotional preparation during seasonal peaks.

## Example SQL Queries

### Monthly Revenue Trend

```sql
SELECT 
    invoice_month,
    ROUND(SUM(revenue), 2) AS monthly_revenue,
    COUNT(DISTINCT invoice_no) AS orders,
    COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS customers
FROM transactions_clean_full
GROUP BY invoice_month
ORDER BY invoice_month;
```

### Revenue by Customer Segment

```sql
SELECT
    segment,
    COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS customers,
    ROUND(SUM(monetary), 2) AS total_revenue,
    ROUND(AVG(monetary), 2) AS avg_customer_value
FROM rfm_segments
GROUP BY segment
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;
```

### Priority High-Risk Customers

```sql
SELECT
    customer_id,
    monetary,
    frequency,
    recency_days,
    churn_risk_score,
    risk_tier,
    recommended_action
FROM churn_scores
WHERE risk_tier = 'High Risk'
ORDER BY monetary DESC
LIMIT 25;
```

### Top Product Revenue Drivers

```sql
SELECT
    description,
    ROUND(SUM(total_revenue), 2) AS revenue,
    SUM(total_quantity) AS quantity_sold,
    SUM(total_orders) AS orders
FROM product_summary
GROUP BY description
ORDER BY revenue DESC
LIMIT 10;
```

### Cross-Sell Opportunities

```sql
SELECT
    antecedents,
    consequents,
    ROUND(support, 4) AS support,
    ROUND(confidence, 4) AS confidence,
    ROUND(lift, 2) AS lift
FROM market_basket_rules
ORDER BY lift DESC, confidence DESC
LIMIT 20;
```
